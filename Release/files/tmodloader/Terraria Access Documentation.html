Terraria Access Documentation

Game Overview

Terraria is a side-scrolling action RPG where the core loop is simple: defeat enemies to get loot, use that loot to defeat harder enemies, and repeat. Wrapped around this loop is a rich sandbox: diverse biomes with unique drops, artificial biomes you can create, and a hostile underground that ranges from cavern layers all the way down to hell.

Terraria Access is a mod designed to make this experience accessible for blind and visually impaired players. Using the Tolk library, it provides universal screen reader support—compatible with JAWS, NVDA, Window-Eyes, SuperNova, System Access, ZoomText, and SAPI as a fallback. The mod adds audio cues and specialized navigation tools. This documentation explains how to use those features effectively, from basic navigation to combat and building.

Installation

Terraria Access now ships with a full installer that handles all file placement automatically. Follow these steps:

  1. Install Terraria and TModLoader via Steam. Open TModLoader once to generate the necessary folders.
  2. Run TerrariaAccessSetup.exe. The installer automatically detects your Steam installation path and places all required files in the correct locations, including the Tolk speech libraries, the mod file, and the recommended input profile.
  3. Make sure a supported screen reader is running before launching TModLoader. Terraria Access supports JAWS, NVDA, Window-Eyes, SuperNova, System Access, ZoomText, and SAPI as a fallback.

Quick Start: Surviving Your First Night

This section will walk you through your first moments in Terraria, from spawning into the world to building your first home. If you want the detailed technical explanations, feel free to skip ahead to the rest of the documentation, but this guide should get you up and running quickly.

First Steps

When you first load up your world, you are going to be thrown into a Forest Biome, surrounded by trees and your Guide NPC. If you want to speak to the Guide, you can use the Guidance System by pressing the Left or Right Brackets to switch to the NPC guidance category, and it will direct you to exactly where he is. When near him, press B on Xbox gamepads, Circle on PlayStation gamepads, or P on keyboard. He will tell you several interesting and useful facts, so definitely walk up to him and speak with him every now and again—it will be worth it.

Gathering Resources

The first thing you are going to want to create is a home. Nothing fancy, just a little shack at this point to avoid being hit by those pesky slimes. Use your number row on keyboard or bumpers on gamepad to swap to your Axe, then enable Smart Cursor by pressing Ctrl on keyboard or clicking down your Left Stick on gamepad.

Once in Smart Cursor mode, walk left and right until you hear "Forest Tree." When you hear this, hold down Right Trigger on gamepad or the I key on keyboard to begin destroying the tree. This might take a moment. Be mindful of those slimes though—they are incredibly annoying. You will hear them creeping up on you with the audio cues they emit.

Once you have broken several trees and feel like you have collected a good amount of wood, you will be ready to build your home. Do not worry if you run out—the world is your oyster, and there are plenty of trees on this oyster. Be mindful though: you may want to plant those acorns that the trees drop, or you will eventually run out.

Building Your First Home

In Terraria Access, you have a few approaches to building. Your best approach would be to use Build Mode, which is a feature unique to this mod. In Build Mode, you can select two points and use either Fill Mode or Outline Mode to build structures or terraform your world. For building a home, Outline Mode is recommended.

Your home needs to be approximately 9 tiles wide and 7 tiles tall in order to house NPCs. Enable Build Mode by pressing Start on gamepad or Z on keyboard until you hear "Build Mode: Outline." If you hear "Build Mode: Fill," press the bind again to cycle to Outline.

Now, place your Unlocked Cursor above ground level. Ground level is generally one tile below your player's feet. Move the cursor around using the Directional Pad or O, K, L, ; keys on keyboard. If no tile-by-tile announcements are happening, press the Smart Cursor button to switch to Unlocked Cursor mode.

Place your cursor somewhere at ground level and press A on gamepad or X on keyboard. This places your first Build Mode point. Now, as you move your Unlocked Cursor around, you will hear announcements like "2 by 3" or "4 by 8"—this is the total width and height of your selection. Since we need 9 wide and 7 tall, move your cursor until you hear that announcement, then press the same button to place your second point.

Next, we need to select our building material. Wood is recommended since it is abundant, but you can use Stone instead. Open your Inventory by pressing Y on gamepad, Triangle on PlayStation, or Escape on keyboard. You should hear that your focus is in your Hotbar. Navigate through the Inventory with the Directional Pad or Left Analog Stick on gamepad, or WASD on keyboard.

If your Wood is already in your Hotbar, you are golden. If not, navigate to your Wood in the Inventory and press A on gamepad, Cross on PlayStation, or I on keyboard to pick it up. Then navigate back to an empty Hotbar slot and press the same button to place it there. Close your inventory.

With your Outline selection set and Wood in your Hotbar, use your number row or bumpers to select Wood, then hold your Place bind (Right Trigger or P on keyboard). You will hear several placement sounds. Eventually, Build Mode will notify you that your blocks are placed. Turn off Build Mode by pressing the same bind until you hear "Build Mode: Disabled."

Entering Your Home

You now have the skeleton of your home—a hollow rectangle. The thing is, we do not have superpowers, so we cannot phase through tiles. Your player model is 3 tiles tall and 2 tiles wide, which means you need to break a three-tile gap in one of your walls to enter.

Switch to Unlocked Cursor mode and navigate around until you hear the Wood you placed. You will probably hear many "Empty" announcements—that is fine. To verify you are on the wall and not the ceiling, move your cursor down one tile. If it says Wood again, you are on the wall. If it says Empty, you were on the ceiling—navigate left or right to find the wall.

Once you find the wall, keep moving down until you hear Grass or Dirt (ground level), then move back up one tile. This is your floorboard. Move up one more tile—this will be the bottom of your door frame. Now equip your Pickaxe from your Hotbar, and with this tile in focus, hold Right Trigger or I on keyboard to break it. Then move up and break the next two tiles above it. You now have a three-tile gap to enter your home.

Take note of the position announcements like "4 right, 2 up"—these tell you how far you have moved from the starting position under your feet. This helps build your mental map, as do the panning and pitching tones you hear when moving the cursor.

Crafting Your First Items

Walk into your new home and open your Inventory. We are going to craft a Workbench, which requires approximately 10 Wood. On gamepad, press Left Trigger to move from your Hotbar to the Crafting List. On keyboard, use Q. Navigate the Crafting List (it is vertical, so use up and down) until you find Workbench. Craft it by pressing A on gamepad or I on keyboard.

Now move the Workbench to your Hotbar. Press Right Trigger or E on keyboard to move back to your Hotbar area, find an empty slot, and place it there. For future reference, both triggers on gamepad or Q and E on keyboard move you through the various areas of your Inventory screen.

Close your inventory. Important note: Crafting Stations and objects in Terraria are not always 1×1 tiles. Workbenches are 2×2, meaning you need a 2×2 empty space to place one. Also, you cannot place them in the air. Move your Unlocked Cursor to just above the floorboards (with the latest mod version, your cursor starts there automatically), then press your Place bind to set down the Workbench.

Completing Your Home

In Terraria, suitable housing requires: a light source, safe walls, a chair, and a table. Your Workbench counts as a table. Let us tackle these one by one.

Chair: Stand near your Workbench, open your Inventory, go to the Crafting List, find Chair, craft it, move it to your Hotbar, and place it next to your Workbench.

Torch: If you have been defeating slimes, you have Gel. Torches require Gel and Wood. If you started in Journey Mode, you begin with 100 Torches. Torches need to be placed on a tile or background wall—place yours on the floor or wall inside your home.

Door: Stand near your Workbench, find Wooden Door in the Crafting List, craft one, and move it to your Hotbar. Go to your door gap, unlock your cursor, and position it one tile above the floorboard on the last Wood tile (the door frame). Doors are three tiles tall and need to connect to tiles on top and bottom. Press your Place bind, and you have a door. Note: you can also place doors with Smart Cursor if you prefer less precision.

Walls: Walls in Terraria are like wallpaper—you can walk through them, but they prevent monsters from spawning inside your home. Stand near your Workbench, find Wood Wall in the Crafting List, and hold the craft button to make many (you will need quite a few). Move them to your Hotbar.

Enable Build Mode and cycle to "Build Mode: Fill." This time, we want to select the space inside the home. Move your Unlocked Cursor to an empty tile in the corner—one tile up from the floorboards and one tile from the wall. Place your first point, then do the same in the opposite corner. Select Wood Wall in your Hotbar and hold your Place bind to fill the interior with walls.

Note: to avoid announcement spam, the mod says "Empty" when moving your cursor without a wall item equipped. If you are holding a Wall or Hammer, you will hear the actual wall tiles instead.

Congratulations—you can finally call this husk a home! Be prepared to build many more throughout your playthrough, because there are a lot of NPCs in this game.

Setting a Waypoint

Before venturing out, create a Waypoint so you can find your way home. Press the Backslash key, type a name for your waypoint, and press Enter. You can navigate to this Waypoint using the Guidance System—press the Left or Right Brackets until you hear "Waypoints," and you will hear your waypoint name along with how many tiles away you are. Use this information and the audio cues to find your way back if you ever get lost. If you are truly lost, press Home to warp to your focused waypoint (this works for every Guidance category).

What Next?

The world is your oyster. You have your home and your waypoint, so feel free to start exploring! Use the Explore Guidance category (accessed with the Brackets) to hear sound cues from key objects around the world. Use Page Up and Page Down to track specific objects and learn both what and where they are.

Note: objects will not emit sounds unless there is sufficient light around them—this matches how sighted players experience the game. There are light-emitting pets you can collect, and torches are abundant, so place one every now and again or you might miss items. When an area is too dark, the mod will tell you. You can also get a Mining Helmet or other gear that makes you essentially a human torch.

Keep this documentation open for reference, read through the technical sections to become a pro, and join our Discord community—we have helpful people who do not mind questions from new players!

Cursor Systems

Smart Cursor

Smart cursor is your main tool for basic navigation and interaction on gamepad. When enabled, it hides the regular cursor and lets the game automatically decide what to interact with:

  • Targets tiles for mining (such as ore and stone).
  • Interacts with doors, chests, and other objects.

You can influence smart cursor by moving the right analog stick. Think of this as a radial selection around your character:

  • If you are mining and it keeps focusing on stone, tilt the right stick toward the ore you want (for example, up to reach iron above you).
  • Whatever direction you tilt the right stick is where smart cursor will look for interactables near your character.

Smart cursor is the "bread and butter" of world navigation and mining on gamepad. It is a big part of why Terraria is playable with a controller and accessible with this mod.

Unlocked Cursor (Tile-by-Tile Mode)

The unlocked cursor lets you move a cursor tile by tile, which is ideal for precise building and targeting:

  • On gamepad, use the directional pad to move the cursor one tile at a time. On keyboard, use O (up), K (left), L (down), and semicolon (;) (right).
  • A tone plays whose pitch changes with vertical position: higher pitch above your character, lower pitch below.
  • The tone also pans left or right according to where the cursor is on the screen.

Your player is roughly three tiles tall. When you unlock the cursor, its starting position is one tile below your character's feet. This makes it significantly easier to quickly build walls for a home, towers to build upward, and other vertical structures. The screen reader will tell you how far the cursor is from that starting position in tiles:

  • "1 right" means one tile to the right of the starting position.
  • "4 right, 1 up" means four tiles right and one tile up from the starting position.

This lets you always know the exact relationship between your character and the cursor, which is crucial for precise building and navigation.

Scanning with the Right Stick in Unlocked Mode

You can also move the unlocked cursor using the right analog stick on gamepad or the arrow keys on keyboard:

  • Directional pad movement (or O, K, L, semicolon on keyboard) is slow and precise, announcing every single tile (for example, "empty, empty, empty" for open air).
  • Right stick movement (or arrow keys on keyboard) is designed for rapid scanning. Duplicate announcements are suppressed, so it only speaks when it crosses a new type of tile.

Example: if you are searching for a rope somewhere in the sky:

  • Put the unlocked cursor somewhere in the upper-left sky area.
  • Hold the right stick to the right to sweep across.
  • When the cursor hits the rope tile, the screen reader announces the rope and its position (for example, "Rope, 7 right, 4 up").

This makes it easy to quickly explore an area and find important tiles, then switch back to precise movement with the directional pad if needed.

Gamepad Emulation Mode

Gamepad Emulation mode, developed primarily by community member Leos (also known as Khai), allows players to navigate and interact with Terraria entirely through a keyboard, providing an alternative to gamepad controls. Press F6 to enable or disable Gamepad Emulation mode.

Basic Movement and Actions

Movement uses the standard WASD keys. Your right hand handles aiming and various other actions:

  • I key: Simulates left click (use items). This is how you fire weapons, break tiles with your pickaxe, and generally use whatever item is in your hand. If you are familiar with gamepad controls, this is equivalent to the right trigger.
  • P key: Simulates right click (interact). Use this to talk to NPCs, open chests, open doors, and place blocks.

Smart Cursor and Virtual Analog Stick

The Control key toggles smart cursor on and off. This toggle affects how the directional keys for your right hand behave:

  • Smart cursor enabled: The directional keys simulate the right analog stick, allowing you to aim in any direction around your character. This is ideal for combat and general exploration.
  • Smart cursor disabled: The directional keys simulate the directional pad, allowing tile-by-tile cursor movement. This is ideal for precise building.

The directional keys for the virtual right analog stick and directional pad are:

  • O: Up
  • K: Left
  • L: Down
  • Semicolon (;): Right

This layout keeps your right hand comfortably positioned so you can quickly switch between aiming and using items.

Inventory Management

When you are in your inventory, several keys help you manage items:

  • Q and E keys: Switch between inventory sections. The inventory screen has multiple sections (inventory, crafting, equipment, etc.), and these keys let you move between them. Press Q to move to the previous section and E to move to the next.
  • F key: Smart select. This versatile key handles multiple inventory actions: quickly moves items between your inventory and an open chest, stores items in nearby chests, sells items when interacting with an NPC shop, and trashes items when no chest is open. Be careful with this last behavior—if no chest is open, pressing F will move the focused item to the trash slot.
  • J key: Quick use. This is how you consume items like Lesser Healing Potions, Night Owl Potions, food buffs (chicken nuggets, cheeseburgers, etc.), and other consumables. Simply focus on the item and press J.
  • P key: Equip armor and accessories. When focusing on an armor piece or accessory in your inventory, pressing P will equip it.

Enemy Targeting

Press the Tab key to target an enemy. This works the same way as targeting on gamepad: the screen reader will announce the enemy's name and health. Press Tab again to untarget.

Main Menu Navigation

When navigating the main menu (Single Player, Multiplayer, Settings, etc.):

  • Use WASD to move through menu items.
  • Use Spacebar to select menu items. Note that the I key does not work for menu selection—you must use Spacebar.
  • Escape acts as the back button, returning you to previous menus.

Workshop Navigation

When browsing mods in the Workshop or Manage Mods screens, press Tab to enter search mode, type your query, then press Enter to perform the search. This allows you to quickly find specific mods by name.

Footsteps & World Traversal

Footstep Chimes & Counting Tiles

Terraria normally does not have any meaningful footstep sounds. Terraria Access adds footstep chimes with real utility:

  • Every time your character passes the center of a tile while walking, a chime plays under your feet.
  • You can count these chimes to know exactly how many tiles you have moved.
  • Footstep pitch changes slightly when walking up or down a tile, indicating elevation changes. A rising pitch means you are going up; a falling pitch means you are going down.

Combined with the unlocked cursor's distance readouts, this lets you position yourself precisely. For example, if a rope is at "7 right, 4 up":

  • Walk right and count seven footstep chimes. You know you are now directly below the rope.
  • Jump or climb to reach it when your cursor says the right vertical distance.

Platforms & Drop-Through Tiles

Platforms are special tiles you can stand on but also drop down through. Terraria Access uses distinct chimes to make them clear:

  • When walking on a platform, you hear a higher-pitched chime. This indicates a tile you can drop through.
  • Platforms are great for building safe mineshafts and sky bridges you can move through vertically.

Hazardous Tiles

Some tiles damage you on contact, such as:

  • Meteorite
  • Certain cactus types
  • Spike traps and similar hazards

Standing on these tiles triggers a special warning sound in the footstep system, letting you immediately know that you are on something dangerous and should move.

Ropes & Vertical Traversal

Ropes are an essential part of vertical navigation. Terraria Access provides:

  • Clear announcements when the unlocked cursor finds rope tiles (for example, "Rope, 7 right, 4 up").
  • Sound effects while you climb, indicating whether you are moving up or down the rope.

Together with tile counts and footstep chimes, rope sounds make it much easier to understand your vertical position in shafts, caves, and other large vertical spaces.

Wall Collision Sound

When you walk into a solid wall, a repeating tap sound plays to let you know you are blocked. The tap pans toward the direction of the wall. This provides immediate feedback that you cannot move further in that direction and need to jump, dig, or change course.

Passage Detection (Gap Sounds)

When falling down a vertical shaft or jumping, the mod detects horizontal passages (side tunnels) that your player could fit into. An ascending chirp sound plays from the direction of each detected opening. This helps you identify cave entrances and branching tunnels without needing to stop and scan with the cursor. Passages must be at least 3 tiles tall (the height of your player) to be announced.

Fall Damage Indicators

While falling, the mod provides audio feedback to help you gauge your descent:

  • A tone plays for each tile crossed during the fall, with pitch indicating proximity to the ground below.
  • After falling past the fall damage threshold (25 tiles), warning beeps play and the screen reader announces "Taking fall damage" to alert you that landing will hurt.

The fall detection mode can be configured in the mod settings to use either tones or beeps.

Underwater & Breath Tracking

When you enter water and begin submerging:

  • The screen reader announces "Breath depleting" when your breath begins to drain.
  • Audio cues play at every 10% breath threshold crossed while underwater, so you can track how much air you have left.
  • When you surface and your breath is restored, the screen reader announces "Breath restored."

Cavity Sonar (Screen Sonification)

Press the tilde/grave key (~) to activate cavity sonar, which sweeps the area around your character and plays tones for empty spaces. The sweep moves from left to right, with each empty tile producing a short pip. The pitch indicates depth (higher pitch for tiles closer to you, lower for tiles further below), and the panning indicates horizontal direction.

This gives you a quick audio snapshot of the terrain around you—revealing caves, tunnels, and open spaces without needing to move the cursor. The sweep completes in about 1.5 seconds. Cavity sonar can be toggled on or off in the mod configuration.

Building & Housing

Player Size & Basic Building Rules

Your player's collision box is:

  • About 3 tiles tall.
  • About 2 tiles wide.

Any area that is at least 3 tiles tall and 2 tiles wide will physically fit your character. This is important for:

  • Designing doorways and hallways.
  • Ensuring your house has enough space to walk around.

Basic building without build mode works like this:

  • Use the unlocked cursor to move next to an existing tile.
  • Select the block you want to place in your hotbar.
  • Press the placement button (right trigger) to place a tile.
  • The screen reader will announce the tile you placed; move the cursor up one tile (now empty) and repeat to build walls upward.

Doors are roughly the same height as the player but need room for hinges:

  • Leave at least three empty vertical tiles for the door itself.
  • Have one tile above and one tile below that space for proper placement.

Build Mode (Extended Placement & Selections)

Build mode is a powerful tool that:

  • Removes the requirement to attach new tiles directly to existing ones.
  • Greatly extends your placement range from about 5 tiles to roughly the entire screen.

Build mode has three modes you can cycle through by pressing the build mode key repeatedly:

  • Fill: Fills the entire selection with tiles.
  • Outline: Places or breaks tiles only around the perimeter of the selection. This is exceptionally useful for building house frames very quickly.
  • Disabled: Turns off build mode.

You can make selections and then fill or outline them:

  1. Move the unlocked cursor to a first point.
  2. Press the build mode selection button to mark this as point A.
  3. Move the cursor to another point.
  4. Press the selection button again to mark point B.

This creates a virtual line or rectangle between points A and B:

  • Holding the placement button fills or outlines the selection with the currently selected block (depending on which mode is active).
  • Using a tool like a pickaxe instead allows you to destroy everything inside the selection, excellent for flattening terrain.

Terraforming with Build Mode

For large-scale flattening:

  • Select from just right and down from your chest to a point near the top right of your screen.
  • Use a pickaxe within build mode to remove hills, trees, and other obstacles inside that region.

This makes it much easier to create flat surfaces for building towns, arenas, or other structures.

Suitable Housing & NPC Requirements

To get NPCs to move into your buildings, each house must qualify as suitable housing. For detailed housing requirements including exact dimensions, visit the Terraria Wiki House page. In general, valid housing includes:

  • Enough interior space meeting minimum size requirements.
  • At least one table (or equivalent surface).
  • At least one chair.
  • At least one door.
  • At least one light source (like a torch).
  • Walls placed in the background (see "Safe vs Unsafe Walls" below).

NPCs provide various benefits once they move in:

  • Nurse: heals you.
  • Dryad: sells seeds and world-altering items.
  • Arms Dealer: sells guns and ammunition.
  • Merchant: sells basic supplies like healing potions.

Safe vs Unsafe Walls

Terraria distinguishes between safe and unsafe wall tiles:

  • Unsafe walls (such as naturally generated dirt walls or dungeon walls) allow enemies to spawn in front of them.
  • Safe walls are player-placed walls (wooden, stone, etc.) and prevent enemy spawns in that area.

When building houses, you should always place safe walls behind your interior spaces so enemies cannot spawn inside your home. Walls are placed in the background only and do not physically block you, so you can think of them like wallpaper.

Tools for Breaking Tiles

Different tools break different things:

  • Pickaxe: used to break almost all solid tiles, including blocks and ores.
  • Axe: used to chop down trees.
  • Hammer: used to break background walls.

The exact tool tiers and breakable blocks are documented on the Terraria wiki and follow the standard Terraria rules.

ChunkMiner (Vein Mining)

ChunkMiner automatically mines entire veins of ore, gems, and other mineable deposits when you break a single tile. Instead of breaking ore one tile at a time, breaking any ore tile will break all connected tiles of the same type in the vein—similar to the popular VeinMiner concept.

ChunkMiner works on:

  • All ores (iron, copper, gold, etc.)
  • All gemstones (sapphire, ruby, emerald, topaz, amethyst, diamond)
  • Silt, Slush, and Desert Fossil deposits

ChunkMiner is enabled by default and can be toggled on or off in the mod configuration. The maximum number of tiles mined per chunk can also be adjusted (default: 100, up to 500).

Guidance System

Overview

The guidance system is your primary tool for finding important objects in the world:

  • NPCs
  • Players (in multiplayer)
  • Crafting stations and interactables
  • Ores and gemstones
  • Chests and life crystals (chest types are spoken—Wooden Chest, Gold Chest, Frozen Chest, etc.; locked chests are announced as locked)
  • Dropped items
  • Critters
  • Plants
  • Hostile mobs

The guidance system covers all ores and gems, many important interactables, all NPCs and crafting stations, dropped items, critters, harvestable plants, and hostile mobs. Each sweep now completes in approximately one second regardless of the number of detected objects, making it more responsive and predictable. Distant entries are filtered out to reduce noise.

Exploration Mode

In exploration mode, the guidance system:

  • Makes nearby trackable objects emit unique sounds (for example, a pickaxe-like sound for nearby ore).
  • Lets you hear multiple things at once so you know you are near something valuable.

Focusing Specific Targets

When exploration mode detects multiple objects, you can cycle and focus on one:

  • Use Page Up and Page Down (by default) to move through the list of detected objects.
  • The screen reader announces the selected object and its position relative to your chest, such as: "Iron Ore, 36 right, 10 down."

Sound cues follow the same logic as other cues:

  • Pan left or right according to horizontal distance.
  • Pitch changes according to vertical position (lower for below, higher for above).

Light Detection & Visibility

The guidance system respects light levels to provide a more authentic experience that matches what sighted players see. This means:

  • Objects in complete darkness will not be detected by the guidance system. For example, an ore vein surrounded by stone with no light source nearby will not emit guidance sounds.
  • You must bring light to an area (using torches, a mining helmet, light-emitting pets, or other equipment) before the guidance system can detect objects there.
  • When you enter a dark area, you will hear an alert indicating you have entered darkness. This is your cue to place a torch or activate a light source.

This change makes the guidance system significantly less overwhelming, as it will not constantly ping ores and objects that you would not normally be able to see anyway.

Some objects naturally emit light and remain detectable even without an external light source:

  • Heart crystals
  • Chests
  • Certain other special objects

Teleporting to Targets

The guidance system supports teleporting to targeted NPCs or crafting stations by pressing the Home key. This feature is optional and can feel powerful; nothing in the mod requires using this teleport to function.

Player Information & Status Feedback

Terraria Access provides player status information through the mod. Press the Select button on gamepad or Backspace on keyboard to hear your current status. Pressing the status button repeatedly cycles through different status categories:

  • Current health
  • Current mana
  • Defense level
  • Current biome
  • Time of day
  • Active buffs

When cycling to the buffs category, all active buffs are read aloud.

Mod Configuration

Terraria Access includes in-game mod configuration. To access these settings:

  1. While in-game, open the Settings menu.
  2. Navigate to Mod Configuration.
  3. You will see a list of all installed mods. Find Terraria Access and select it.

Within the Terraria Access configuration, you can adjust:

  • Whether a tone plays when smart cursor is enabled.
  • The volume of footstep sounds.
  • Various other mod-related sound settings.
  • Edge detection mode: Off, Static (continuous white noise that increases as you approach a cliff edge), or Beeps (high-pitched tones near edges).
  • ChunkMiner (vein mining) toggle and max tiles per chunk.
  • Cavity sonar toggle.
  • Fall detection mode (Tone or Beeps).
  • Wall collision sound toggle.
  • Passage detection toggle.
  • Heartbeat health indicator toggle.

This gives you fine-grained control over your accessibility experience without needing to edit configuration files manually.

Workshop Support

Terraria Access provides accessibility support for the TModLoader Workshop, allowing you to browse and download mods directly from within the game.

Accessible Workshop features:

  • Download Mods: Browse and download new mods from the Workshop.
  • Manage Mods: Enable, disable, and organize your installed mods.
  • Mod Packs: Browse and manage mod packs.
  • Develop Mods (Mod Sources): Navigate the mod sources screen for building and managing source projects.

When navigating Workshop or Manage Mods screens, press Tab to enter search mode, type your search query, then press Enter to perform the search. This allows you to quickly find specific mods by name.

Bestiary

The Bestiary menu is now fully accessible. The Bestiary is Terraria's encyclopedia of creatures you have encountered. From this screen you can:

  • Browse the grid of creatures, with each entry announced by name.
  • Sort and filter entries using accessible sort and filter controls.
  • Search for specific creatures by name.
  • Navigate between pages of entries.

Access the Bestiary from the main menu or the in-game pause menu.

UI & Menu Accessibility

Interface Audio Cues

Terraria Access provides audio feedback when navigating the inventory and other interfaces. As you move focus between items:

  • Audio cues pan left or right based on the horizontal position of the focused item on the screen.
  • Audio cues adjust pitch based on the vertical position—higher pitch for items near the top, lower pitch for items near the bottom.

Additionally, interface sections are announced with prefixes to help you orient yourself. These prefixes include:

  • Inventory: Your main item storage slots.
  • Hotbar: The quick-access bar at the top of your inventory.
  • Crafting List: The list of available recipes.
  • Crafting Grid: The grid showing craftable items.

These audio cues and prefixes make it much easier to understand where you are within the interface without needing visual feedback.

Inventory navigation has been improved: previously unlabeled buttons and confusing elements in the inventory screen now have proper labels and announcements, making the entire inventory experience more reliable.

First-Letter Navigation

While in your inventory or a chest, press Tab to toggle first-letter navigation mode. When enabled, pressing any letter key (A–Z) cycles through items whose names start with that letter. This makes it fast to find specific items in a large inventory without scrolling through every slot.

Controls Menu Scrolling

The Controls menu behaves differently than typical menus:

  • Once you reach the apparent bottom, you cannot move further just by pressing up or down on the directional pad.
  • You must use the right analog stick (or O, K, L, ; keys in Gamepad Emulation mode) to scroll down further and reveal more options.

Character Creation & Customization

Hairstyles & Clothing Descriptions

On the character creation screen:

  • All 165 hairstyles have been given custom audio descriptions. Simply use left and right to navigate through all hairstyles—no scrolling required.
  • All 10 clothing variants (base outfits) are also described.

This is intended to give you a clear mental picture of how your character looks, avoiding accidental "random goofball" appearances unless that is what you want.

Color Selection via Codes

Character colors (hair, eyes, shirt, pants, shoes, etc.) are handled using a slider-based system in vanilla Terraria that is not screen-reader-friendly:

  • Hue
  • Saturation
  • Brightness

Instead of making these sliders accessible, Terraria Access:

  • Makes the color code fields accessible, where you can paste specific color codes.
  • Lets you obtain color codes from external sources (for example, a website) and paste them in so you know exactly which color you are choosing.

World Creation

The world creation screen is generally straightforward with screen reader support:

  • You can choose the world evil: Corruption, Crimson, or Random. This determines the main hostile biome.
  • Select the world size (small, medium, large).
  • Pick the world difficulty (Classic, Expert, Master, etc.).

For more information about how worlds are generated and what to expect in each biome, visit the Terraria Wiki World Generation page. One strong recommendation:

  • If you are playing single player, choose a small world to make navigation much easier.

Biomes & Music Feedback

Terraria Access announces biome transitions:

  • "Entering Snow Biome"
  • "Entering Forest Biome"
  • "Entering Desert Biome"
  • "Entering Beach Biome"
  • "Entering Space Biome"
  • "Entering Hell"
  • "Entering Underground"
  • "Entering Cavern Layer"

In addition to these announcements, Terraria's music changes based on biome. Each biome has its own track, providing another way to confirm where you are.

World Events & Chat

Terraria includes many world events, such as:

  • Blood Moons
  • Lantern Nights
  • Parties
  • Lunar Eclipses
  • Goblin Invasions
  • Pirate Invasions

All of these world events are announced through the standard narration pipeline.

Chat messages are automatically read aloud as they appear. Press Enter to open the chat and type a message. To review older chat messages, use Page Up and Page Down to scroll through the chat history. This includes player messages, system announcements, and notifications about players joining and leaving in multiplayer.

Combat

Basic Combat on Gamepad

On gamepad, combat generally works as follows:

  • Aim using the right analog stick with a ranged weapon (bow, gun, etc.).
  • Press the right trigger to attack or shoot.
  • For precise targeting with ranged weapons, use the lock-on feature by pressing X on Xbox controllers or Square on PlayStation. On keyboard, press Tab to lock onto an enemy.

Many early melee weapons, such as the starting copper shortsword, do not require precise aiming and simply swing in front of you.

Enemy Audio Cues

Enemies emit sound effects that:

  • Pan left or right based on their position relative to you.
  • Change pitch based on vertical position (above or below).

Tracking these sounds alongside your footstep chimes makes it easier to know how close enemies are and from which direction they are approaching.

Targeting & Enemies

To automatically target enemies:

  • Gamepad: Press X on an Xbox controller or Square on a PlayStation controller.
  • Keyboard: Press the Tab key.

The screen reader announces the enemy's name and its current health. This makes it much easier to:

  • Keep track of which enemy you are focusing on.
  • Monitor its remaining health during combat.

Auto-Fire & Weapon Types

In the in-game settings, enabling auto-fire is highly recommended:

  • Instead of repeatedly tapping the right trigger, you hold it down and attack at the fastest rate allowed for that weapon.

As you progress, you will obtain bows and guns that work very well with auto-targeting, making combat feel smooth and manageable. There is also a summoner class archetype that largely removes the need to aim at all, since minions attack automatically.

Menus Requiring Scrolling

Remember that the Controls menu requires scrolling once you reach the bottom of the initially visible options. On gamepad, use the right analog stick. On keyboard in Gamepad Emulation mode, use the O, K, L, ; keys. Without scrolling, additional options will not appear.
